Conventioner MVP: the journey completes, but the product will not let you read it

A second full walk of the organizer journey - create, plan, import, review, assign, publish, check in - plus the public vendor surface and the signed-out screens. The pipeline is sound and the new triage and import screens are good work. What stops this being shippable is a handful of layout and colour defects that make finished screens unreadable, and a publish model expressed by five different labels that disagree with each other.

Build fix/authorization-integrity @ 29b1a632 Stack conventioner-dev (fe :5273 / be :5090) Market QC Winter Market 2026 · Nov 21-22 · Gold/Silver · 12 tables/day Widths 1440x900, 1280x720, 390x844 Date 15 Sep 2026

The short version

The journey completes end to end. I created a market through the UI, planned dates / tiers / locations / sections, built the application form, imported a five-row CSV, triaged every application, ran the solver, published, and checked a vendor in on a phone. Every step worked. The import reconciliation screen and the one-at-a-time review queue are genuinely well designed.

One screen is functionally broken. The Tables view - the thing an organizer reads on market day - clips 1,942px of its 2,762px of content inside an overflow:hidden box that does not scroll. On my two-day, 24-table market you can see six tables. The second day is unreachable.

Two design-system colours fail WCAG AA, and one renders white on white. --mm-grey, the hint-text colour, renders at 1.66:1. --mm-green, on every primary button in the product, renders at 2.65:1. And the phase panel's "Current Phase:" label is rgba(255,255,255,.7) on a white page - invisible, which is why the phase strip reads as four unlabelled pills.

Publishing is five different words. "Done", "Publish Results", "Publish Market", "Begin Market Days" and "Archive Market" all appear in the flow. Two of them are the same transition under different names; one of them - the primary CTA on the payoff screen - fails with a raw enum error in the phase the organizer is actually in when they reach it.

Everything below was verified in the running build with measurements or a reproduction, not inferred from source. Where I checked a suspicion and it turned out to be correct behaviour, I have said so rather than reporting it.

Blocking defects

Ship-stoppers: a screen that cannot be read, and a primary action that fails
B1

The Tables view clips 70% of its content and cannot be scrolled

TablesView.vue · /markets/:marketId/tables
Blocker

.tables-card is overflow:hidden with a fixed height, and .tables-body inside it is overflow:visible. Nothing scrolls. The document itself scrolls 45px and stops. On a two-date market with 12 tables per day, the organizer sees Front Row 1 through 6 of day one and nothing else - the whole of 22 November is rendered into the DOM and painted outside the clip.

Measured in the running build .tables-card clientHeight 820, scrollHeight 2762, overflow-y: hidden · .tables-body clientHeight 671, scrollHeight 2684, overflow-y: visible · document.scrollHeight 945 vs clientHeight 900.
Why it matters This is the screen an organizer opens at the door on market day to find out who sits where. It is also the only place the per-table assignment is visible in the product. On any market larger than a handful of tables it shows the wrong answer by omission, silently.
Fix Move the scroll to .tables-body (overflow-y:auto; min-height:0) and let .tables-card be a flex column with the header and actions row fixed. The same fixed-viewport-height card pattern is what leaves Attendance Status as a 820px white slab holding 200px of content, so fix both together.
B2

"Done" - the primary CTA on the payoff screen - fails with a raw enum error

GenerateAssignmentView.vue handleDone() · guards.py VALID_TRANSITIONS
Blocker

The natural journey is: open applications, import, review, click Assign on the setup screen, land on Assignment Results, click Done. handleDone() posts toPhase: 'market_days'. But Assign never moves the phase, so the market is still applications_open, and market_days is only reachable from assignment or offers. The request 400s.

The real route is applications_closed → review → assignment → market_days, four manual clicks in the unlabelled phase strip at the top of the page. Nothing in the wizard mentions the phase strip; nothing in the phase strip mentions the wizard. They are two parallel, unconnected models of "where am I in this market".

Why it matters The first-time organizer follows the wizard to its last button and is told no, in language written for the database. There is no recovery instruction on screen.
Fix Two parts. (a) Make Assign advance the phase, or have handleDone() walk the intervening transitions itself. (b) Never render a raw phase key: the transition endpoint already knows the display labels the phase strip uses ("Applications Open", "Begin Assignment") - use them, and say what to do next.

Systemic: three colour values that break the whole product

Each is a one-line change with product-wide reach
S1

The hint-text colour renders at 1.66:1 - every helper line in the product is illegible

front-end/src/assets/base.css:8 · --mm-grey: rgba(39,35,35,.25)
Critical

On white, rgba(39,35,35,.25) composites to roughly #C9C9C9. Contrast against the page: 1.66:1. WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 even for large text. The token is used as a color: value in 18 places across the app, and as a border or background in 57. Corrected 19 Sep: this first read “65 places”, a count that matched border-color: and background-color: too. The token is overwhelmingly a border colour that 18 sites borrow as text, which is what makes the fix cheap: the border uses keep their value.

WhereWhat is in that colourConsequence
CSV import, step 2The FIRST ROWS sample valuesThe data you must read to decide the mapping
CSV import, step 1"Nothing is written until you confirm."The reassurance that makes the step safe to take
CSV import, step 3The CSV header names in "Where each answer comes from"Half of a two-column mapping table
Applications tab"No applications received yet."The only content on an empty screen
Attendance"No check-ins recorded yet."Ditto
Import gateThe entire explanation of why import is blockedThe only thing on the page
Fix One line. The alpha must reach about 0.64 for the composite to hit #767676, the standard AA-passing grey on white: --mm-grey: rgba(39,35,35,.64). Keep the 0.25 value under a separate token for borders and dividers, which is the other half of its 160 usages and where it is perfectly fine.

(Also at that line: --mm-black is declared twice in the same block, lines 3 and 6.)
S2

Every primary button's label is white on brand green at 2.65:1

--mm-green: #49b096 · every .primary-button, every CTA, the check-in button vendors tap
Critical

Measured on the live Check in button: foreground rgb(255,255,255), background rgb(73,176,150), 16px - 2.65:1. The same green is also used as a text colour on white (the date headings on the check-in cards, 18px), where it is the same 2.65:1.

Fix Keep #49b096 as the brand fill, but darken the variant used behind white text and as text on white. --mm-green-dark: #2f7f6a already exists in these review artifacts and lands at 4.6:1 for white text. Two token additions, no layout change.
S3

"Current Phase:" is white text on a white page

PhaseControlPanel.vue · the label is rendered, positioned and invisible
High

The phase strip has a label. It is at x:40, y:117, 93x22px, in rgba(255,255,255,.7). .phase-control-panel is background: transparent and its nearest painted ancestor is rgb(255,255,255). The panel was styled for a dark surface and is rendered on a light one.

Why I went looking I read the panel's textContent and it said "Current Phase:Applications ClosedReopen ApplicationsBegin ReviewArchive Market" - but no screenshot at any width showed the words "Current Phase". That is what led to the measurement.
Fix Give the label an ink colour. While you are in there, the panel needs to separate state from action - the current-phase pill should not be the same shape and size as the buttons beside it - and the three transition buttons need consistent colour semantics: green currently means both "Close Applications" (forward) and "Reopen for Editing" (backward).

High: defects that cost the organizer real work

Six issues found by walking, each reproduced
H1

A new market skips the Market Dates step entirely

MarketSetupView.vue:157 · localStorage.getItem('setupPageIdx')
High

I created a market, chose "Manual setup", and landed on Tier Setup - step 2. Step 1, Market Dates, was never shown. I only found it by pressing Back twice.

The cause: setupPageIdx is a single global localStorage key with no market id in it. Whatever step you last stood on in any market becomes the opening step of the next one. The first market an organizer ever makes is fine; every market after it opens wherever they left off.

Why it matters Market dates are the foundation of everything downstream - the solver assigns per date, the essential question "which dates can you attend" is generated from them, and the import's date reconciliation offers them as its only valid values. A market that quietly has none produces an assignment of zero.
Fix Key the stored step by market id, or simply always open at step 0 for a market whose setupObject.marketDates is empty.
H2

Organizer screens are keyed on a localStorage "current market", not the URL

/market-setup, /import-applications, /assignment-results, /vendors - none carries a market id
High

Four of the six organizer routes take no parameters. They read localStorage.getItem('market'). Three observable consequences, all reproduced:

  • Deep links and refreshes break. After signing out and back in I loaded /market-setup directly. It rendered a page titled "Settings" with an empty Market Dates panel, a live Next button, and no phase strip - an editable setup wizard attached to no market.
  • The drawer's "View Vendors" lies. It reads as a global vendor directory. It renders the vendors of whichever market you last opened, and its heading names that market. From a fresh session it shows "No market loaded."
  • Two tabs interfere. Opening a second market in a second tab moves the first tab's market under it.
Fix Put the market id in the path, as /markets/:marketId/tables and /markets/:marketId/attendance already do. If that is too large for this release, at minimum guard the no-market case: redirect to /markets rather than rendering an editable empty wizard.
H3

The importer asks you to map the product's own values to themselves

csv_import.py · value reconciliation compares against stored keys, not displayed labels
High

My CSV carried A whole table to myself, Half a table, shared and Either is fine in the Table choice column. Those are verbatim the three options the product's own form preview shows the applicant. The importer reported all three as "did not match your market" and offered, as the resolution for each, the identical string.

The same screen shows a second instance. A single column mapped to a multi-value target is split on commas, with no way to change the delimiter - and Google Forms date labels contain commas. "Saturday, November 21, 2026; Sunday, November 22, 2026" became six fragments: Saturday, November 21, 2026; Sunday, November 22, 2026, Sunday. Meanwhile the values it offers as the valid targets are 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-22, which is a format the product shows the organizer nowhere else.

Corrected 19 Sep, after tracing the importer The splitting half of this was largely my fixture's doing. The importer has two shapes, and it only splits when a single column is mapped to a target; a grid - several columns, one per option, with the option in the header - is never split. The real Fall 2025 export uses the grid (columns 20-24, one per date), so the organizer's actual file never hits this. I wrote a single column holding semicolon-separated long-form dates, which is not a shape Google Forms produces for that question. The case is still producible by a checkbox question whose option labels contain commas, and Google's own export is genuinely ambiguous there, so it earns a warning rather than a parser - but it is not on the common path. The ISO-vs-label half stands untouched, and so does the table-choice key mismatch above it.
Why it matters The import screen says "Bring in the responses your Google Form collected." A multi-date checkbox column from a Google Form is the single most likely thing to arrive here, and it is guaranteed to shred.
Fix Three separate things: compare against the label as well as the key; offer market dates by their display label, not ISO; and let the organizer choose the split delimiter (or detect semicolons, which is what Google Forms actually emits between checkbox answers when the answers themselves contain commas).

I checked two adjacent suspicions and both turned out to be correct behaviour, so I am not reporting them: the limited auto-mapping is a documented decision in suggested_mapping() with a stated rationale, and "Section preference" is correctly absent as a target because a ranking of one section is not asked.

"Publish Results" is enabled on a market with zero applications and zero assignment, and nothing on screen says what "results" means. H5

The payoff screen does not say why two vendors went unassigned

GenerateAssignmentView.vue · "Unassigned Vendors (2)"
High

My run: 5 applications, 5 approved, 3 assigned, 19 of 24 table-slots free, two vendors unplaced. The screen lists the two email addresses under a heading and says nothing else. The reason is that both asked for Silver and my only section is Gold - which is knowable, and is exactly the kind of mistake an organizer makes when planning tiers.

Three smaller things on the same screen:

  • Two columns are empty and stretched. Per Date and Per Section are sized to the height of Unassigned Tables, so the organizer scrolls through roughly 1,300px of blank white to reach the bottom of the page.
  • Tables sort lexicographically. Front Row1, Front Row10, Front Row11, Front Row12, Front Row2, Front Row3 ... Table 2 is fifth in the list.
  • "Satisfaction Score 60.0%" has no definition, no breakdown and no tooltip anywhere in the product. On a run with no applications it reads 0.0%, which looks like a bad result rather than no result.
Fix The solver already knows why each vendor failed - surface it per row ("no Silver table on any date they chose"). Give each statistics column its own scroll or let it size to content. Sort table names naturally.
H6

Bulk approve is absent, and the review panel resizes under the cursor as you work

ApplicationMonitor.vue · the triage queue
High

The one-at-a-time triage queue is good: keyboard shortcuts, a running tally, an honest amber note, and a "Show N reviewed" list with a working reversal. But there is no bulk approve control, which was named in E04's scope, and its absence bites hardest in exactly the case the amber note describes - "every application reads alike and there is nothing here to tell applicants apart". The seeded 232-vendor market would be 232 clicks.

Reject is also full-width-third, the same visual weight as Approve, and positioned first in reading order. On a repetitive click task with a panel that jumps 15px on the first action, that is a misclick waiting to happen.

Fix Reserve the scrollbar gutter so the panel width never changes. Add the bulk action. Consider making Reject secondary-weight.

Medium: polish, consistency and dead ends

Grouped by kind
M1

Setup rows: a squashed delete icon, an overflowing drag handle, and selects that render blank

ElementTierSetup / ElementLocationSetup vs ElementSectionSetup
Medium

Three defects visible in one screenshot, on the rows an organizer uses to build every tier, location and section.

  • The delete icon is crushed. icon-close-round declares 24x24 and renders 8x20 inside a 10px flex cell on tier and location rows. The same icon on a section row renders correctly at 24x24 - the two sit side by side on one screen.
  • The drag handle overflows. Its SVG is 16x56 inside a 29x38 container, so the grip lines are clipped top and bottom and read as a rendering error.
  • Selects render completely blank. A fresh section row's Location and Tier dropdowns have selectedIndex: -1 - the "Select a location" placeholder option exists but is not selected, so the control shows nothing at all, has no border, and does not read as a control.
  • It is not a button. The delete control is a <div> with cursor:auto, tabIndex -1, no role and no accessible name, in a 10px-wide hit target.

The identical selectedIndex: -1 bug appears on the CSV import mapping screen: every unmapped column's "MAPS TO" select is blank rather than showing "Ignore this column", because columnTarget[index] is undefined rather than ''.

M4

Dead ends and dead controls

Import gate, 404 page, public header
Medium
  • The import button on a draft market leads to a wall. The Applications tab offers a prominent "Import from CSV" on a draft market; clicking it navigates to a page that only says no. The gate is correct - the button should have been disabled with the reason, before the click.
  • And the wall's advice is wrong for the state that reaches it. "Move the market back to applications closed and you can import again." From draft the organizer needs to move forward, and applications_closed is not among the two transitions the phase strip offers them. The message is written for one direction and shown for both.
  • The import wizard has no exit. No Cancel, no "back to market", no breadcrumb at any of its four steps until the final confirmation. The only way out is the browser or the hamburger.
  • The 404 has no way home. "Page not found. There is nothing to see at this address." - no link, and the organizer hamburger still in the header.
  • The public header's hamburger is a dead control. Signed out, on the vendor check-in page, the menu button renders and is clickable and there is no nav element in the DOM at all. It does nothing.

I verified that /qc-winter-market-2026 returning "Page not found" for a live CSV-intake market is the documented intake-mode gate working correctly, not a bug. It is worth noting as a UX consequence though: a vendor who trims /check-in off the URL they were given is told the market does not exist.

M6

Silent value clamping, and a percentage with no ceiling on the way in

ElementAssignmentOptions.vue
Medium

I typed 99 into "Max assignments per vendor" and it became 2 - the number of market dates - with no message. I typed 150 into "Max half table proportion per section (%)" and it became 100 on blur, also silently. Both fields are type="text" with no min/max, so there is no numeric keyboard on a phone and no browser-level validation either.

Neither field has help text, a placeholder, a default, or an explanation of what a "half table proportion" is - and the Assign button is disabled until both are filled, so the organizer must guess at two undefined numbers before they can proceed.

M7

Two save models on one panel, one of them silent

MarketSetupView.vue → EssentialFieldsPanel
Medium

Unticking "Ask this" on Section preference fires PUT /markets/:id/application-form immediately. I confirmed the 200 in the network log. Nothing on screen acknowledged it - no "Saved", no spinner, no change of state. Six inches away, custom fields require an explicit Save Form click with a status message.

The organizer has no way to know which of their changes are already persisted. (I initially read the disabled Save Form button as "this change cannot be saved" - it can, by a different path, which is precisely the problem.)

The Form Builder is otherwise the best-designed screen in the product: the essential-questions explanation, the live applicant preview and the amber note about table types are all excellent. Its two panels are 484px and 519px tall inside a 900px window with 300px of empty page below them, though, so both scroll when they need not.

M8

Sign-in screen: three words for one action, and a sign-out that keyboards cannot reach

LoginView.vue, NavBar
Medium
  • Login / Sign in / Login - tab, heading and button, three labels for one action on one screen, in a product that says "Sign out" elsewhere.
  • Placeholder-only labels, including Register's "Password (min 8 characters)" - the rule vanishes the moment you start typing it.
  • The submit button is a full pill while every other button in the product is a rounded rectangle, and the fields between them are square.
  • "Forgot password?" is Material blue #2196F3, the only blue in the product, at 3.14:1 on white.

What is working, and working well

Verified, not assumed
  • The import value-reconciliation screen. Naming every unmatched value, counting the rows that carry it, and refusing to advance until each is resolved is exactly right. The "Required questions" checklist that turns green as you map is the best single piece of feedback in the product.
  • "Nothing is written until you confirm" - and it means it. The dry run, the per-row preview cards and the "Where each answer comes from" table make a risky step safe.
  • The one-at-a-time triage queue with R / S / A shortcuts, a live tally, and a reversible "Show N reviewed" list.
  • The Form Builder and its live applicant preview. The essential-questions explanation and the amber note about table types are honest, specific and well written.
  • The setup-path chooser. A clear recommendation, an honest BETA badge, and a caveat that says exactly what the beta does not do yet.
  • Phone check-in. Stacked cleanly at 390px, full-width targets, readable. E08/F03 landed.
  • The intake-mode gate. A CSV market's public applicant surface answers exactly as a nonexistent one does. I tried to catch it out and could not.
  • The solver. Correct placements, correct per-date tier handling, correct refusal to place vendors whose tier does not exist.
  • Zero console errors across the entire authenticated journey except the two deliberate 4xx responses.
  • "Total tables" updates live as you edit section counts.

Suggested order

Cheapest high-reach work first
  1. Three colour values S1, S2, S3 - one line each, and between them they fix legibility on every screen in the product including the public one. Do these first; they are the highest ratio of reach to effort in the list. base.css, PhaseControlPanel.vue
  2. Make the Tables view scroll B1 - a functional blocker on the market-day screen, and the same fix covers Attendance Status. TablesView.vue, AttendanceStatusView.vue
