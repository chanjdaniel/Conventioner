# Brain dump - 2026-10-19: manual UX/UI testing

Informal capture from a manual UX/UI testing session, in whatever order it comes.
Nothing here is decided or scoped.
A later pass groups the ideas into topics, extracts pure fixes and epics into `.scratch/backlog/` (or open questions into `.scratch/wayfinding/`), and marks each idea below with where it went.

Three kinds of item, kept apart:

- **Improvements and changes** - changes to existing code and behaviour.
- **Future features to explore** - new ground, not yet understood well enough to build.

### Topic 1: Every step leads to the next

-> [E32 Every step leads to the next](../backlog/E32-every-step-leads-to-the-next/epic.md)

**Ideas.** The mission, again; Begin Review; Import still offered in review; Begin Assignment; Publishing to Attendance; the Google Form path has no collecting step; the CSV path has no public form (from the sign-in code entry).

**Draft destination.**
On each intake path, the organizer always sees one obvious next step, it is refused until what must come first is done, and taking it lands them on the page where that next step's work happens.
The two paths are distinct sequences: a Google Form market never shows a public form, a preview, or a collecting step it does not have.

**The fog.**

- Does the CSV path keep `applications_open` and `applications_closed` with a different face, or lose them? The 2026-10-18 dump said the phases stay.
- What a forward step does after it moves the phase: navigate always, or only when the current page belongs to the phase being left?
- Late responses on the CSV path: once importing is done and review begun, can a second export still come in?

### Round 1

1. **The Google Form path's stages** (Topic 1): the organizer sees Plan, Import, Review, Assign, Market days. The server's phases stay as they are. Leaving draft reads "Import responses" and takes the organizer to the import; "Form Closed" is never shown, because Begin Review chains importing -> closed -> review in one move, as the form amendment chains.
2. **Every rail move lands on the new phase's page**, forward or back: the page the guide's table names (`currentPage`). Begin Review, Begin Assignment and Publish are one fix.
3. **A blocked next step offers its fix as the main button** ("Import responses", "Add a location"), with the move shown disabled beside it.
4. **Begin Review on the Google Form path needs at least one import**: a new guard in `guards.py`, "Import your Google Form's responses".
5. **The preview is a full-width step inside the tab**, not a new browser tab: a draft market has no public page to open.
6. **A Google Form market's form tab has three steps**: essential questions, extra fields (proposed from the CSV), review setup. No preview; the mapping stays in the import, where the file is.
7. **After the form locks, the tab opens on Review setup**, with steps 1-3 reachable read-only. The step header is clickable at all times; Next is the obvious path, not the only one.
8. **No question is ever inferred required**: a form started from a CSV proposes every question not required. The 97% guess silently skipped real rows at import.
9. **"Needs follow-up" is a new stored status.** It blocks Begin Assignment as an undecided application does, and the applicant sees "under review". The one-by-one queue leaves it out by default; the bulk view filters for it. Marking one opens the vendor's notes to say what is needed (optional). **Amended:** every verdict records who gave it ("approved by", "rejected by"), and a follow-up records **who is responsible** for it.
10. **Bulk verdicts: approve, reject and follow-up**, on checkboxes within a filtered list, with a confirmation naming the count. A row opens the full application. Nothing acts on "all matching the filter". This reverses real-market-readiness 04's "no bulk action", on the organizers' request.
11. **Several reviewers: a claim and a conditional write, both.** The queue skips applications another reviewer holds, and a claim expires after a few idle minutes; a verdict is written only if the application is still as the reviewer saw it, and otherwise says who changed it. Every verdict records its reviewer and time. A bulk verdict skips claimed or changed applications and says how many.
12. **An automatic reject is a real rejection** with its reason stored ("Answered No to the no-show agreement"), overturnable by a reviewer. Choice answers only for now. Rules apply to undecided applications as they arrive and again whenever a rule changes, never touch a verdict a person gave, and their reason is shown to the organizer, not the applicant.
13. **One vendor details drawer**, opened from Vendors, a seat in the results, an attendance entry and the bulk review.
14. **The drawer has tabs**: Application (the submission and notes, as today), Assignments, History, with the notes pinned above the tabs.
15. **On Attendance, the vendor's name opens the drawer**; check-in stays its own large target. A row shows notes only when it has some, and a first note is added from the drawer.
16. **Change placement opens the results with the search set to the vendor and the date to that placement's**. The search covers preferred name, full name, email, business email, business name, and the table label.
17. **"All others"**: a multi-select answer scores by its best-placed choice; a blank answer sorts last, behind the lowest-placed answer.
18. **The CSV download keeps the chosen name and adds a "Full name" column.**

See also [the Google Form path has no collecting step](#the-google-form-path-the-collection-is-already-done-so-importing-is-the-step), which goes further.

### Production: the applicant's sign-in code never arrived

-> E32/F03 (S01 the sign-in page, S02 the send, S03 test markets, S04 the doubled email)

Seen once in production, so it may be a one-off; investigate whether the flow is broken.
After starting a market from a Google Form upload and opening it, the organizer opened the market's public form, gave their email when asked, and pressed send.
No email ever arrived.

Two explanations from the code (`request_code` in `back-end/api/applicant_auth.py`, read 2026-10-19), not yet checked against production:

1. **By design, on a Google Form market.** A CSV market's applicant surface is gated (`applicant_intake_market_by_slug`), and the code request answers with the same 200 "sent" whether or not the market takes applications, so as not to reveal which slugs are real. For a CSV market nothing is sent. The flow worked as built, but the organizer was shown a "sent" for a send that never happens.
2. **A real production bug, on any market.** The email is sent from a daemon thread after the response returns. On Vercel's serverless functions, the process may be frozen once the response is out, so the thread may never finish the send. That would break the sign-in code for form markets too.

**Checked 2026-10-19 (Resend and Vercel, read-only).**
No applicant sign-in code email has ever reached Resend from production, to anyone: Resend's API logs show no `POST /emails` at all after the organizer's own OTP at 2026-10-18 06:26 UTC.
Both explanations leave exactly that evidence, so it does not settle which.
Explanation 1 is the likely one: a market started from a CSV upload is a CSV market unless its intake mode was switched, and a CSV market's sign-in page shows no market name (its form fetch 404s).
Explanation 2 is untested rather than ruled out: the daemon-thread send has never once been shown to deliver on Vercel, so it is a live risk the first time a form market takes a sign-in.
Vercel's Hobby plan keeps runtime logs for one hour, so the request itself is gone.
To settle it: check the market's `intakeMode`, then request a code against a known form market and read the Vercel logs within the hour.

Seen in passing: two "Verify your email address" emails to one address 150 ms apart on 2026-10-18, which looks like a double-submitted signup.

**Whichever it is, on the Google Form path the flow should not be reachable at all.**
Keep the two paths distinct: a Google Form market does not need the form preview or a public form.
It needs the form definition and the import mapping.
The preview, and everything about what an applicant sees, belongs only to a market whose vendors fill the form in on its own page.
Ties into [the four steps of the Application Form tab](#application-form-tab-four-steps-one-at-a-time-with-the-preview-out-of-the-way), which then differ by path, and into how the organizer reached a public form link on a CSV market in the first place.

### Check: the "All others" answer in an assignment priority rule

-> E34/F05/S01

Asked to double-check that it works as intended.

**Intended meaning.**
Every answer the organizer has not placed in the ordering shares the position where "All others" sits, at equal priority.
Example: on "Which clubs are you a member of?", the organizer orders **All others** first and **I am not part of any of these clubs** second.
Every club is then equal first priority, and an applicant who chose "not part of any" comes last.

**What the code does** (`_arranged_score` in `back-end/assignment/assignment.py`, read 2026-10-19).
An answer that is placed scores its own position, and an unplaced answer scores the position of "All others", so the example works as intended for a single answer.
Applicants tied there are separated by the next rule, then by the most-constrained-first tiebreakers.
Pinned by `test_an_answer_the_organizer_did_not_place_falls_to_all_others` in `back-end/tests/test_assignment_behaviour.py`.

**Two edges worth deciding, likely on exactly this kind of question:**

- **A multi-select answer is scored by its first choice only** (`_priority_answer`). An applicant who ticked "Club B, Club A" under an ordering of `Club A, All others, None` scores as Club B, so "All others", not first. A best-placed-choice reading may be what organizers expect.
- **A blank answer falls into "All others"** too, since it is not a placed answer. In the example, an applicant who left the clubs question blank ranks equal first with the club members, ahead of "not part of any". Whether blank should sort last instead is a decision.

Whether a Google Forms checkbox question reaches the solver as a list, or as one comma-joined string (which would never match a placed answer and always fall to "All others"), should be checked against the real export too.

### Automatic rejections: answers that reject an application, with a reason

-> E33/F03

A new section of the Application Form tab (step 4 of [the four steps](#application-form-tab-four-steps-one-at-a-time-with-the-preview-out-of-the-way)).
The organizer specifies which questions, and which answers to them, make an application an automatic reject.
In the review stage those applications are already rejected, with the reason given.

Examples from the real form:

- **The no-show acknowledgement.** "I understand that no-shows without notice will not receive a refund, and no-shows without justification may be banned from participation in future events." Anything but yes is an automatic reject.
- **Affiliation.** An applicant who is not a UBC student, alumnus, or holder of another accepted affiliation is an automatic reject.

To think about later (the automatic rejections): whether the rule matches chosen answers only (choice questions) or also free text, how the reason is worded to the organizer and to the applicant, whether the organizer can overturn an automatic reject, and how it applies to a CSV import as well as to online applications.

### A bulk review view, beside the one-by-one review

-> E34/F04

A request from the organizers.
In addition to the one-by-one review, a bulk view: every applicant and their review status on one page, where rejections and approvals can be managed quickly.
Clicking into an applicant shows their information.

Note for the later pass: this reverses a settled decision.
[real-market-readiness 04](../wayfinding/real-market-readiness/issues/04-what-a-reviewer-needs-to-decide.md) chose triage with **no bulk action at all**, so that no application is ever approved without being looked at, and superseded the v0.1.0 bulk-approve ticket.
Real organizers now ask for it, so that ruling is reopened by evidence rather than overlooked.

### Several organizers reviewing at once

-> E34/F03

A team of organizers will do the reviews, at the same time and from different places.
Think through the races and conflicts: what happens when two verdicts on the same application arrive at the same time, or two reviewers walk the same queue.

What the code does today (read 2026-10-19):

- A verdict is an unconditional write (`update_application_status` in `back-end/api/applications.py`): the last one wins, silently, and nothing records who gave it.
- Each reviewer's queue of undecided applications is their own browser's copy, so two reviewers starting together walk the same cards in the same order and duplicate each other's work.

Idea to explore: a claim, or soft lock.
When a reviewer moves to the next application, the system checks whether anyone else has already claimed it or is looking at it, and skips to one nobody holds.
Questions to answer: how a claim expires (a reviewer who walks away), whether a verdict on an application someone else already decided is refused, warned about, or allowed, and whether the verdict should record its reviewer.

Also think about how the [bulk review view](#a-bulk-review-view-beside-the-one-by-one-review) plays in: a bulk verdict over applications another reviewer currently holds, and a bulk list that goes stale while others decide.
And the [needs follow-up status](#review-replace-skip-with-needs-follow-up): who owns the follow-up.
