# Manual QC walk - 2026-09-21

Findings from a manual QC session, dictated by Daniel.
Documentation only: nothing here is implemented yet.
Each finding is recorded as reported, with any clarification added inline.

It splits two ways, the `E09`/`E14`/`E15` way: **Findings** have exactly one right answer and
become an epic, **Open questions** are decisions about what a screen should do and get a Wayfinder
map first.

## Shared vocabulary

Agreed 2026-09-21 so a finding names one thing and only that thing.
Every term below is the one the source already uses, except where a collision forced a choice.

| Term | What it is | In the code |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **the market workspace** | the whole page an organizer works a market from | `front-end/src/views/MarketSetupView.vue`, route `/market-setup` |
| **the settings panel** | the white container holding everything below the market name | `.settings-container` |
| **the tab bar** / **a tab** | the four buttons: the Application Form tab, the Market Setup tab, the Applications tab, the Assignment Results tab | `.tab-bar`, `market-setup-<name>-tab` |
| **the phase rail** / **a phase** | the lifecycle band under the tab bar, on every market screen; its steps are `draft`, `applications_open`, `assignment`, `archived` | `front-end/src/components/PhaseRail.vue` |
| **the plan** | what the Market Setup tab edits: the market's dates, tiers, locations, sections, priority and options | `.settings-body-plan`, `setupObject` |
| **a plan card** | one of the six: the Market Dates card, the Tier Setup card, the Location Setup card, the Section Setup card, the Assignment Priority card, the Assignment Options card | `ElementSettingContainer`, grouped by `.plan-row` |

Two rules this buys us:

- **"Market Setup" now names the tab, never the page.** The page is the market workspace.
  `MarketSetupView.vue` keeps its filename for now; renaming it is a separate, optional change.
- **The tabs are not states.** Market state lives in the phase rail and nowhere else, so "the
  states at the top" must not be used for the tab bar.
- **"Section" keeps its domain meaning** - a physical area of the market floor - which is why the
  six are *cards*, not sections.

## Findings

### 01 - Sign in with a code: the two inputs sit too close

**Screen:** organizer sign-in, "sign in with a code" (OTP) form, after clicking **Send a code**.
`front-end/src/views/LoginView.vue` - `login-otp-email-input` and `login-otp-code-input`.

**What happens:** once the code step appears, the email input and the 6-digit code input are
stacked with too little space between them. Focusing the code input draws its focus highlight
into the email input above it, so the two controls' rings overlap.

**Expected:** a normal field gap between the two, wide enough that neither field's focus ring
touches its neighbour.

### 02 - The top bar must never scroll away

**Screen:** every page. `front-end/src/App.vue` - the `<header>` wrapping `ElementBanner`.

**What happens:** the top bar (logo + navigation menu button) is an ordinary in-flow element at the
top of the shell, so on any page tall enough to scroll it scrolls off and the navigation menu
becomes unreachable without scrolling back up.

**Expected:** the top bar is visible at all times, on every page, regardless of scroll position,
and behaves identically everywhere - authenticated screens and the public pages alike.

**Constraint:** `.app-container` is deliberately `min-height: 100vh` and grows past the viewport so
the PAGE scrolls (`E16/F03`). Do not reintroduce a viewport-height shell with an internal scroller
to achieve this; the fix belongs on the header itself.

**Adjacent question, not yet decided:** whether `PhaseRail` should stick beneath the top bar too,
or scroll away with its screen.

### 03 - Create-new-market modal: the name field does not read as a field

**Screen:** the create-new-market overlay. `front-end/src/views/NewMarketOverlay.vue` -
`new-market-name-input`.

**What happens:** the market name input shows only its example placeholder (`Winter Market 2026`)
with no visible control around it. There is nothing that says "you type here" - it reads as a
suggested title rather than as an editable field.

**Expected:** the name input is unmistakably an input on first sight, before anyone clicks it - its
own border, fill and height - so the example name is understood as a hint about what to enter
rather than as the value.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:** the field is stripped twice over.
`.text-input-container input` is `all: unset`, which removes the border, background, padding and
height the browser supplies; and `.text-input-container` around it declares
`border-radius: var(--radius-card)` with **no `border` and no `background`**, under a comment
reading "a field is a border (E16/F02)" - the comment states the intent but the declaration was
never written. So the control renders as bare text on the dialog.

A label *does* exist and is correctly associated (`<label class="field-label" for="new-market-name">
Market name</label>`), styled `--text-xs`, uppercase, `--mm-text-muted`. Whether it survives the
redesign in Q3 is that question's to answer; the missing affordance is the field itself, not the
label.

**Constraint:** `.field` in `front-end/src/assets/primitives.css` owns control height, padding,
radius, type, focus and the disabled state. Reach for it rather than re-deciding locally (`E16`) -
and note that `all: unset` on the input will defeat the primitive too if it stays.

### 04 - The settings panel has no outer gutter

**Screen:** the market workspace, on the Market Setup tab.
`front-end/src/views/MarketSetupView.vue` - `.settings-container` inside `.market-setup-body`.

**What happens:** the settings panel - the white container holding the tab bar, the phase rail and
the six plan cards - runs flush to the edges of the workspace.
There is nothing between the panel and the page on the left, the right or the bottom.

**Expected:** a gutter on the left, right and bottom of the whole panel, so it reads as a card
sitting on the page rather than as the page itself. Top is deliberately not in scope - the panel
meets the top bar above it.

**Note for whoever implements:** the *inner* padding already exists - `.settings-body` is
`padding: 40px`. This finding is about the outer gutter around `.settings-container`, not that.
The gutter must come from a spacing token, and it applies to the panel on every tab, not just the
Market Setup tab.

### 05 - Market Dates: the date picker opens on the left of the field

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Market Setup tab -> the Market Dates plan card.
`front-end/src/components/elements/ElementMarketDates.vue` - `setup-dates-date-input-<n>`.

**What happens:** clicking a date row opens the browser date picker anchored to the LEFT of the
row. It should open on the RIGHT of the date display.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:** the row is a formatted `.date-display` heading with a
native `<input type="date">` laid invisibly over the whole row (`position: absolute; left: 0;
width: 100%`, transparent text, `onclick="this.showPicker()"`). The popup is drawn by the browser
and anchored to that input's own box, so it follows the input's left edge - which is currently the
left edge of the entire row. The popup's position cannot be set in CSS; it has to come from where
the input sits. Moving or narrowing the input must not break clicking anywhere on the row to edit,
which is what the full-width overlay currently buys.

### 06 - Plan cards clip the shadow off their own rows

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Market Setup tab. Every plan card.
`front-end/src/components/elements/ElementSettingContainer.vue` - `.setting-body` and the
`::v-deep(.row-container)` rule.

**What happens:** the editable rows inside a plan card carry `box-shadow: var(--shadow-card)`, and
that shadow is cut off by the card body that contains them. The rows read as clipped rather than
as raised.

**Expected:** a few pixels of buffer on all four sides inside the card body, so a row's shadow
renders whole.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements - there are two causes and the obvious fix only addresses one:**

1. **`.setting-body` is a scroll container** (`overflow-y: auto`), so anything painted outside its
   padding box is clipped. Its padding is `15px` top, `20px` left/right, `20px` bottom.
   `--shadow-card`'s widest layer is `0 6px 14px`, which paints roughly 8px above a row, 20px
   below it and 14px to each side - so the top is definitely short and the other three are at best
   exactly flush. Whatever buffer is chosen has to clear the shadow's real extent, not a guess.
2. **The rows are 8px wider than the space they have.** `::v-deep(.row-container)` sets
   `margin-left: 8px; margin-right: 8px; width: calc(100% - 8px)` - margins plus width come to
   `100% + 8px`, so every row overhangs its container's right edge by 8px and is clipped there
   regardless of padding. That looks like the arithmetic was meant to be `calc(100% - 16px)`, or
   the margins were meant to be `4px`. Fix this first; padding alone will not rescue it.

**Constraint:** this is one shared component, so the fix lands once and reaches all six plan cards.
Check the Section Setup card specifically - it is the widest and the one most likely to be already
scrolling horizontally because of cause 2.

### 07 - Assignment Priority card: the add control is not centred

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Market Setup tab -> the Assignment Priority plan card.
`front-end/src/components/elements/ElementAssignmentPriority.vue:333-336` - `priority-add-rule`.

**What happens:** the `+` icon and the words "Add a rule" are not centred horizontally on the card,
and they are not aligned with each other.

**Expected:** the icon and its label sit together on one line, centred horizontally on the card, as
one control.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:** `.add-row` and `.add-round` have **no CSS rules anywhere in
the repo** - the component defines `.icon-add-round` (40x40, pointer) but the markup applies
`class="add-round"` to `IconAddRound`, so the icon gets no sizing and the row gets no layout. The
row is therefore a bare block containing an inline SVG followed by a block `<h3>`, which is why the
label drops below the icon and both sit hard left. The other row-based plan cards centre their add
control via `.add-container` (see `ElementMarketDates.vue`); this card should reach for the same
treatment rather than inventing a third.

### 08 - Manage organization: adding or removing a member closes the modal

**Screen:** Organizations -> Manage on an organization.
`front-end/src/views/ManageOrgOverlay.vue` - `handleAddAdmin`, `handleAddMember`,
`handleRemoveUser`.

**What happens:** adding an admin, adding a member, or removing a user all succeed and then
immediately `emit('manageClose')`, so the modal disappears. To see the result the organizer has to
reopen it, and to add two people they have to reopen it between each one.

**Expected:** the modal stays open and shows the updated membership in place. Closing it stays an
explicit action.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:** `emit('manageClose')` is doing double duty here. The parent
(`OrganizationsView.vue:74`) treats close as its refresh signal - `handleManageClose()` clears the
selection and calls `loadOrganizations()` - so closing the modal is currently the only thing that
re-reads the data. Keeping the modal open therefore needs two separate things: the overlay
refreshing its own `orgData` after a successful mutation, and the parent list still learning about
the change (a distinct event, or a refresh on the real close).

**Precedent in the same file:** `handleRename` already does this correctly - it updates
`orgData.value` locally on success and does not close. Follow it.

**Three closes here are correct and must stay:** deleting the organization, the explicit close
button, and the Escape/background dismiss. Only the three membership mutations change.

**Test impact:** `front-end/e2e/pages/OrganizationsPage.ts` and the org specs assume the modal
closes after add/remove. Those expectations move with the behaviour; check
`addMemberSubmit` / `removeMemberButtons` call sites.

### 09 - Phase rail: the current step's dot crowds its label

**Screen:** the phase rail, on every market screen. `front-end/src/components/PhaseRail.vue` -
`.phase-step`, `.phase-step--current .phase-step-dot`.

**What happens:** the current phase's dot is filled and given a black ring, which makes it visually
larger than every other dot on the rail - but the space between dot and label does not grow with
it, so the ring sits almost against the text.

**Expected:** in the current state only, a slightly larger gap between the dot and its label, so
the ringed dot has the same breathing room the plain dots have.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:** `.phase-step` sets `gap: 6px`. The current dot's ring is
`outline: 2px solid var(--mm-black); outline-offset: 2px`, and an outline is painted **outside the
border box without taking part in layout** - so it consumes 4px of that 6px gap and leaves about
2px of visible space. The dot is 9px + 1.5px border for every step; only the current one wears the
extra 4px.

The gap has to grow by the ring's full extent (4px) on `.phase-step--current` alone, not on the
rail as a whole. Growing it everywhere would leave the ordinary steps looser than they are now, and
the walk found no fault with those.

**Watch for:** `.phase-step + .phase-step` adds `border-left` and `padding-left: 10px`, so the step
separators sit close to the dots too. Confirm the ring is not also crowding the divider to its
left before settling on a number.

### 10 - Plan cards: centring and the add-row plus need one treatment, not six

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Market Setup tab. All the row-based plan cards.
`front-end/src/components/elements/Element{MarketDates,TierSetup,LocationSetup,SectionSetup,AssignmentPriority}.vue`.

**The ask:** across every plan card, (a) whatever should be centred actually is, and (b) the `+`
icon that adds a row is consistent in size, spacing, padding and position.

**What the source shows - the plus has no owner anywhere.** A sweep of the five row-based cards:

| Card | Wrapper | Icon class | Wrapper styled? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market Dates | `.add-container` | `icon-add-round` | no rules, in any file |
| Tier Setup | `.add-container` | (no icon class read) | no rules |
| Location Setup | `.add-container` | (no icon class read) | no rules |
| Section Setup | `.add-container` | (no icon class read) | no rules |
| Assignment Priority | `.add-row` | `add-round` | no rules |

`.add-container` appears in four components and is **styled in none of them**. Where the plus looks
centred today it is centred by accident: the parent `.rows` is a flex column with
`align-items: center`, which shrink-wraps the unstyled wrapper and centres it. Nothing states the
intent, so nothing protects it.

`.icon-add-round { width: 40px; height: 40px; cursor: pointer }` is then **declared five times,
identically, in five files** - the definition of a primitive that was never extracted. Assignment
Priority is the one that drifted: its markup says `class="add-round"`, which matches nothing, so
that card's icon is unsized and its label falls below it (this is finding 07, and it is a symptom
of the table above rather than a one-off).

**What this wants:** one add-row control - wrapper and icon together - owning its own size,
padding, gap and alignment, used by all five cards, with the centring stated on the control rather
than inherited from whichever parent happens to be a centred flex column. `.btn` in
`front-end/src/assets/primitives.css` is where that belongs (`E16`).

**And separately, an audit:** walk each card and check what is *meant* to be centred - column
headings against their columns, the values in a row against their headings, the remove control,
the add control - rather than assuming the flex defaults got it right. The Market Dates row already
centres its date via `.date-display`; nothing says the other four agree.

**Related:** finding 07 is the one card where this has already broken visibly. Fixing 07 alone
leaves the other four centred by coincidence.

### 11 - Opening a market always lands on the Market Setup tab

**Screen:** the market workspace. `front-end/src/views/MarketSetupView.vue:47` (`tabFromRoute`).

**What happens:** opening a market always lands on the Market Setup tab. `tabFromRoute()` reads
`?tab=` from the route and falls back to `'setup'` unconditionally - it never looks at the market.

**Expected:** the landing tab follows what the market still needs, so an organizer returning to a
half-built market is put where the work is.

**This finding is downstream of Q5** (the stage order). The landing rule cannot be written until
the order of stages is settled, because the rule *is* "land on the earliest unfinished stage" and
Q5 decides what those stages are and how each one is known to be finished. Do not implement 11
before Q5 resolves.

Two things hold regardless of how Q5 lands:

- **An explicit `?tab=` in the URL must still win**, so a shared or bookmarked link keeps working.
- **There is no stored signal for "finished" on the application form today.**
  `ApplicationFormObject.published_at` is the field that would mean it - declared in
  `back-end/datatypes.py:567` as "locks form when applications exist (D9)", threaded through
  `api/markets.py` (lines 181, 264, 1362), and read by `PhaseRail.vue:92` and `FormBuilder.vue:40`
  - but **nothing in the back end ever assigns it a value.** It is only carried from the stored
  form into the response, so it is `None` on every market in the product. Whatever Q5 decides,
  something has to start writing it, or "finalized" has to be derived from something else.

### 13 - Add-a-field editor: the Label / Key / Type labels are not vertically centred on their inputs

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Application Form tab -> the add-a-field editor.
`front-end/src/components/application/FormFieldEditor.vue` - `.field-row`, `.field-label`,
`.field-input`.

**What happens:** the `Label`, `Key` and `Type` captions to the left of their controls do not sit
on the vertical centre line of those controls.

**Expected:** each caption is optically centred against the control it names.

**What the source already says, so the obvious fix is not the fix:** `.field-row` is
`display: flex; align-items: center`, so the label *box* and the control *box* are already centred
against each other. The misalignment is therefore **inside** the boxes, not between them - adding
`align-items: center` again will change nothing. Two candidates, and they should be measured rather
than guessed at:

- **The label has no `line-height` and the control has an explicit one.** `.field-input` is
  `height: 28px; padding: 2px 8px; border: 1px`, so with the global `border-box` its text is
  centred in a 22px content box. `.field-label` is `font-size: var(--text-xs)` with whatever
  `line-height` it inherits. Two boxes centred against each other still put their *text* on
  different lines when the line boxes differ. CLAUDE.md already records this exact trap for
  controls: "a control's height is its line box", which is why the `font` reset pairs with
  `line-height: normal`.
- **`Type` is a `<select>` wearing `.field-input`.** A select given an explicit height plus padding
  and no `line-height` centres its text by its own UA rules, which do not match a text input's -
  so `Type` may be off by a different amount than `Label` and `Key`. Check the three separately
  before assuming one fix covers all of them.

**Also in this editor, same rule:** the `Required` row is `.field-row-checkbox`, which overrides
only `gap` - its control is a bare checkbox with no `.field-input` height at all, so it centres
differently again. And the `Options` row pairs the caption with `.options-list`, a flex *column* of
rows; centring a caption against a stack of several rows is a different question from centring it
against one control, and the walk should decide what it should line up with (probably the first
row, not the stack's midpoint).

### 14 - The drag handle is two hairlines at 25% opacity

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Application Form tab -> the field list. The grab handle beside
each added field. `front-end/src/components/icons/IconClickDrag.vue`, used by
`front-end/src/components/application/FormBuilder.vue:123` (`form-builder-drag-handle`).

**What happens:** the control an organizer drags to reorder fields is barely visible and does not
read as a grab affordance.

**Expected:** a clear, conventional drag handle that says "pick me up" before anyone hovers it.

**The icon, in full - it is eleven lines and explains itself:** `IconClickDrag.vue` is an 8x28 SVG
containing **two vertical lines** at `stroke-width: 0.8`, wrapped in `<g opacity="0.25">`, stroked
`#222222`. Two 0.8px hairlines at quarter opacity is close to invisible on white, and two parallel
lines are not the shape any interface uses for this - the convention is a grid of dots or a stack
of horizontal grip lines, which reads as texture to grip rather than as a divider.

**Three things to fix together:**

1. **The shape.** Replace the two lines with a recognisable grip.
2. **The weight.** `opacity: 0.25` on a 0.8px stroke is below the threshold where a control
   announces itself. `.drag-handle` separately sets `color: var(--mm-text-muted)`, which the icon
   ignores - it hardcodes its own `#222222` instead, so the handle cannot be themed or given a
   hover state from CSS. Make the icon inherit `currentColor` and let `.drag-handle` own the
   colour, including a darker hover.
3. **The target.** `.drag-handle` is `padding: 10px 4px` around an 8px-wide icon - a 16px-wide hit
   area for a drag control.

**Blast radius:** `IconClickDrag` is used in three places - `FormBuilder.vue`,
`ElementTierSetup.vue` and `ElementAssignmentPriority.vue` - so one icon change reaches every
reorderable list in the product. Check all three; the plan cards are narrower than the form
builder's field list and a bigger handle costs more there.

**Related:** the hardcoded `#222222` is one of the 351 hardcoded hex colours `E16` exists to
retire. Take the token while the file is open.

### 15 - Form builder: nothing delineates one added field from the next

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Application Form tab -> the field list.
`front-end/src/components/application/FormBuilder.vue` - `.field-item`, `.field-card`.

**What happens:** consecutive added fields run together. There is not enough separation to see where
one field ends and the next begins.

**Expected:** each added field reads as its own unit at a glance.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements - the spacing actively works against grouping:**
`.field-item` sets `margin-bottom: 8px` *between* fields, while `.field-card` sets `gap: 6px`
*within* one. Eight against six is no difference at all: a row of the same field and the start of
the next field are the same distance apart, so proximity gives the eye nothing to group on. The gap
between fields has to be clearly larger than the gap inside one - that relationship is the fix, not
the absolute number.

**And `.field-card` is not a card.** It declares only `flex: 1; display: flex;
flex-direction: column; gap: 6px` - no border, no background, no padding, no radius. Each "card" is
an unbounded stack of rows. Whether the answer here is spacing alone or an actual bounded card is
worth deciding deliberately, since `docs/design-system.md` already owns the card idiom (10px
radius, the three-layer shadow) and the essential-questions panel next to it *does* bound each
question (`.essential-item` has a border, radius, padding and a white ground). The two halves of the
same screen currently disagree about whether a form field is a card.

**Noticed while reading the same file, not asked for:** `.field-type-badge` is
`--mm-text-muted` on a `--mm-border` fill, which computes to **3.73:1** - below AA for the
`--text-xs` it is set in. This is the exact failure CLAUDE.md warns about: `--mm-border` is exempt
from `contrast.test.ts` because it "never carries text", and here it carries text. `.required-badge`
beside it is fine (white on `--mm-red`).

### 16 - No way to reach or share the public application URL

**Screen:** the phase rail, on the market workspace (Application Form tab in particular).
`front-end/src/components/PhaseRail.vue`.

**What happens:** nothing in the organizer's view exposes the market's public application URL
(`/<market-slug>/apply`). An organizer cannot copy it to send to vendors, and cannot see what an
applicant sees.

**Expected:** a single compact control in the phase rail, to the right of the Close Applications
button, that both **copies the URL** and **opens the application page**. Visible in **every phase**
- before applications open, while open, and after they close - so the organizer can always check
what a visitor gets.

**There is already a pattern to follow, three lines up.** The rail has a `checkin-chip`
(`phase-rail-checkin`): a label, the URL as a link, and a Copy button that flips to "Copied" for
two seconds (`copyCheckInUrl`, `PhaseRail.vue:139`). The application chip should be that control,
not a new idea. Note the check-in chip appears **only** in `market_days` - "Only a published market
has one. Before that it would be a link to a 404" - which is the opposite of what is wanted here,
so the visibility rule is the one thing deliberately not copied.

**The thing to decide, because it makes this more than a button:** *what does the applicant page
show in each phase?* The ask is that the organizer sees what a visitor sees, in all three states -
so the value of the control depends entirely on the applicant page having a real answer for "not
open yet" and "closed". If it 404s or shows an empty form in those phases, the button ships a link
to a broken page. Check `front-end/src/views/ApplicationPage.vue` against each phase before
building the control.

**And the blocker: intake mode.** `applicant_intake_market_by_slug()`
(`back-end/market_documents.py:361`) gates every applicant endpoint on `intakeMode === 'form'`, and
**absence means `csv`** - deliberately, so the applicant surface fails closed. A CSV market's
`/apply` URL answers exactly as a market that does not exist. CLAUDE.md also records that MVP ships
no UI control for intake mode, because every MVP market is CSV. So today this button would offer
every organizer a link to a 404.

That gives three options, and one has to be chosen before the control is built:

1. **Show the chip only for `intakeMode: 'form'` markets.** Correct, and invisible in MVP.
2. **Ship the intake-mode control too**, so an organizer can choose. CLAUDE.md says withholding it
   is deliberate ("a toggle would advertise a surface MVP withholds"), so this is a product
   decision, not a UI one.
3. **Decide MVP markets should take applications by form**, which is a much larger change.

**Do not** add a "this market does not accept online applications" message on the public page as a
way around it - CLAUDE.md is explicit that a gated market must answer exactly as a market that does
not exist, so a distinct message tells any stranger guessing slugs that the market is real.

### 17 - Save Form button: wrong alignment, wrong type size

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Application Form tab, the save row under the field list.
`front-end/src/views/MarketSetupView.vue:594-601` - `form-builder-save-button`, `.form-save-row`,
`.done-button`.

**What happens:** the Save Form button sits at the bottom **left** of the form builder, and its
label is too large.

**Expected:** aligned bottom **right**, with slightly smaller text.

**Mechanism, for whoever implements:**

- **Alignment.** `.form-save-row` is `display: flex; flex-direction: row; align-items: center` with
  **no `justify-content`**, so it defaults to `flex-start`. Note the row also holds the save status
  messages (Saved / the error / the hint), which currently trail the button on the right. Moving
  the button right has to decide where those go - most likely the status stays left and the button
  is pushed right by `margin-left: auto` on the button, which keeps the messages readable at the
  start of the row rather than crowding the button.
- **Type size.** `.done-button` is `font-size: var(--text-lg)`. The scale in
  `front-end/src/assets/base.css` documents `--text-lg: 20px` as "section headings, card titles"
  and `--text-sm: 14px` as "body, form fields, **buttons**, list rows". So the button is two steps
  off what the scale says a button is; `--text-sm` is the value the design language already names.

**Blast radius - `.done-button` is shared.** It is also the plan's save button
(`MarketSetupView.vue:845`) and both action buttons on Assignment Results
(`AssignmentResults.vue:635,645`, the download and Discord buttons). Dropping `font-size` on the
class changes all four. That is very likely correct - all four are buttons and the scale says
buttons are `--text-sm` - but it is a four-screen change, not a one-button change, and each should
be looked at. The alignment fix is local to `.form-save-row` and affects nothing else.

**Related:** `.btn` in `front-end/src/assets/primitives.css` is meant to own button type, height and
padding (`E16`). `.done-button` re-deciding `font-size`, `min-height`, `padding` and `font-family`
locally is the pattern that primitive exists to replace - worth folding in if the file is open
anyway.

### 18 - Preferred name should be an essential question

**Contract:** `back-end/essential_fields.py`, mirrored by
`front-end/src/utils/essentialFields.ts`.

**The ask:** add **preferred name** as an *essential* question - explicitly **not** a required one.

**That distinction is exactly the one this contract already draws.** "Essential" means a question
the product owns and every form asks; "required" means an applicant cannot submit without
answering. They are separate lists, and `TABLE_SHARE_EMAIL_KEY` is already essential-but-optional -
`REQUIRED_ESSENTIAL_KEYS` names seven of the nine essential keys and deliberately omits it, with
the reason recorded in place. Preferred name takes that same shape: asked of everyone, answerable
by nobody without consequence.

**The history, which matters here.** `readable-journey` ticket 02 (built as `E13`, PR #70) chose
`essential_full_name` and *saw this column*: the committed Fall 2025 export carries **Full Legal
Name** and **Preferred Name** as columns 4 and 5, and the ticket says outright that Conventioner
"drops both because it has nowhere to put them." It then built a home for column 4 only. What it
explicitly deferred was a *trading name* (`Paper & Pine` rather than `Ana Rivera`) - a different
thing from a preferred name, and the deferral does not cover this.

So this is a real gap, not a reopened decision: the acceptance fixture has the data, the contract
has no key for it, and the product shows people their legal name.

**Sub-decisions this needs before it can be built:**

- **Which name does a screen show?** This is the whole point of adding it. `VendorIdentity.vue` is
  the one component every vendor-facing screen renders through, so the rule lands in one place:
  presumably preferred name when present, full name otherwise. Say it once, there.
- **Is it gated on anything?** Every essential question except the name is gated on the plan
  offering something (dates gate availability, tiers gate tier preference). The ticket records that
  `full_name` is "asked unconditionally, which is a first". Preferred name should follow it - gated
  on nothing, since identity does not depend on the plan.
- **It stays out of `SOLVER_RELEVANT_KEYS`.** Same reasoning the ticket gives for `full_name`:
  correcting a spelling must not invalidate a completed review.
- **It is not addable to `UNASKABLE_ESSENTIAL_KEYS`.** That list admits **rankings only**, and the
  comment there states why - a default on a non-ranking invents a commitment the applicant never
  made. An optional question does not need to be declarable-unasked anyway.
- **CSV import must be able to map it**, or the Fall 2025 export's column 5 still has nowhere to go.

**No migration needed, for the same reason `E13` needed none:** `_solver_vendor` names the keys it
reads explicitly rather than looping over `asked_essential_keys()`, and stored applications are not
re-validated - so an existing application with no preferred name keeps assigning. An optional key
makes this even safer than `full_name` was.

**Mechanics:** adding an essential key changes the contract, so regenerate `docs/schema.d.ts`
(`python generate_market_schema.py --output ../docs/schema.d.ts` from `back-end/`, pinned by
`tests/test_generate_market_schema.py`), and keep `front-end/src/utils/essentialFields.ts` in step -
CLAUDE.md requires the two mirrors not drift.

### 19 - CSV mapping: let the form be edited from here, without the round trip

**Screen:** Import applications from CSV -> step 2, Map columns.
`front-end/src/views/CsvImportView.vue` - the rail, around `import-declare-unasked`.

**What happens:** when the mapping reveals the form is wrong - a question it never asked, a
question it should stop asking - the wizard cannot fix it. It prints instructions instead. The rail
literally says: *"Reopen the market for editing, turn it off in the form builder, then open
applications and import again."* Four steps, two phase transitions, and the organizer loses their
upload and their partial mapping on the way.

**Expected:** a button here that opens a modal to edit the form in place, and handles the phase
transitions legally and invisibly. The organizer never learns that a phase moved.

**The constraint is already written down at the point of pain.** The comment above that rail block
says: *"Turning a question off is a change to the FORM, and a form is editable only in draft (D9).
This wizard only ever runs in applications_open, so it points at where to do it rather than
offering a button that would be refused here."* So the instructions are not laziness - they are an
honest dead end given the rules. This finding is the request to remove the dead end, not to
discover it.

**What makes it feasible:** the round trip is *legal*, just manual. `("applications_open",
"draft")` is a real edge in `VALID_TRANSITIONS`, guarded only by `_NO_APPLICATIONS_YET` - and
during mapping no application exists yet, because none have been imported. So the seamless version
is: drop to `draft`, `PUT /markets/<id>/application-form`, return to `applications_open`, all
orchestrated behind one modal.

**Four things that will break it if unhandled:**

1. **`FormHasFieldsGuard` gates the way back.** `("draft", "applications_open")` requires the form
   to ask something. If the edit empties it, the return transition is *refused* and the market is
   stranded in `draft` - mid-import, with the organizer given no hint that the phase moved. The
   modal must validate against that guard before it commits anything, not after.
2. **The wizard's state must survive.** The uploaded file, the resolved column mapping and the
   cursor position all have to be intact when the modal closes, or this replaces a four-step round
   trip with a two-step one.
3. **Going to `draft` takes the public application page off the air.** Harmless for a CSV market
   (its applicant endpoints are gated shut anyway - see finding 16), but if intake mode ever
   becomes settable, a form-intake market would silently close to applicants for the duration.
   Decide now whether this control is offered at all on a form-intake market.
4. **It only works before the import commits.** The instant one application exists, D9 freezes the
   form for good and `_NO_APPLICATIONS_YET` blocks the way back - the guard's own docstring says
   so: "Once one application exists the market cannot return, and the form is frozen for good."
   The modal must be unavailable after step 4, and must say why rather than failing.

**Related:** the rail has a second dead end of the same shape - the unmapped-column case in the
ledger, which the comment calls out as "the same shape". Whatever is built here should cover both,
or the wizard keeps one dead end and loses the other.

## Policies

Product-wide rules agreed during this walk. Each applies everywhere, not to one screen, so each
needs an audit pass rather than a single edit. Finding 02 (the top bar never scrolls away) is a
policy of this kind too, recorded as a finding because it has one implementation site.

### P1 - Enter submits

**The rule:** wherever an input sits with a confirm or submit button, pressing Enter in that input
performs the same action as the button. This holds everywhere in the product, not only where it was
noticed.

**Where it was noticed:** the Manage organization modal - rename, add admin, add member
(`front-end/src/views/ManageOrgOverlay.vue`). None of its three inputs responds to Enter today.

**Scope, from a sweep of the front end.** Three different situations exist and they want different
answers:

1. **Native `<form>` already handles it** - `LoginView.vue`, `PasswordResetRequestView.vue`,
   `PasswordResetView.vue`, `ApplicationPage.vue`, `AttendanceCheckinView.vue`, `FormPreview.vue`.
   Nothing to do; a submit button inside a form gets Enter for free.
2. **Hand-rolled and already working** - `NewMarketOverlay.vue` and `OrganizationsView.vue` use
   `@keydown.enter`; `ApplicantLogin.vue` and `SectionGrouping.vue` use `@keyup.enter`; the
   floorplan panels (`TableTypePanel`, `TemplatePanel`, `ScaleCalibration`) use `@keydown.enter`,
   two of them with `.prevent`. These work but disagree with each other - **pick one form and make
   them agree**, so the next author copies a single pattern.
3. **Missing entirely** - `ManageOrgOverlay.vue` (three inputs), `ManageMarketOverlay.vue`,
   `PlacementDialog.vue`, `CsvImportView.vue`. These are the actual work.

**Two things the implementation must not get wrong:**

- **Enter must respect the same guard the button does.** Every one of these handlers already
  refuses an empty or invalid value (`if (!orgData.value || !newAdminEmail.value.trim()) return`),
  and Enter must go through that same function, not around it - otherwise Enter submits what the
  button would have refused.
- **A disabled submit means Enter does nothing.** Where the button is disabled (the new-market
  submit with no organization chosen, for instance), Enter must be inert too.

**Open sub-question:** what Enter should do in a multi-line textarea, and in a row-based plan card
where Enter might reasonably mean "add another row". Neither is settled here; the rule above is
about a single-line input paired with a confirm button.

## Open questions

These are not findings. Each is a decision about what a screen should *do*, which a token scale or

### Q1 - What is the right UI for choosing market dates?

**Screen:** the market workspace -> Market Setup tab -> the Market Dates plan card.
`front-end/src/components/elements/ElementMarketDates.vue`.

**The tension:** the Market Dates card sits alone on its row (`.plan-row--single`), so it gets the
full width of the settings panel - but it displays one date per row and grows downwards. A market
with several dates is a tall narrow column of single values stranded in a very wide box, while the
card's own width does nothing.

**The question:** what should displaying and selecting a set of market dates look like in a space
that is wide and short rather than narrow and tall? A calendar with the market's days marked, a
horizontal run of date chips, a compact range picker with exceptions, or something else - and does
the answer differ for a two-day market and a twelve-day one?

**Related:** finding 05 (the date picker opens on the left) is about today's row-based control. If
this question resolves towards a different control, 05 may be answered by the rewrite rather than
fixed on its own - so check this before implementing 05.

### Q2 - What happens to a market when its organization is deleted, and when may an organization be deleted at all?

**Screen:** Organizations -> Manage -> Delete organization.
`back-end/api/organizations.py:138` (`delete_organization`), `back-end/app.py:478`.

**What happens today - the whole of it:** the only precondition is that the caller is the
organization's **owner**. There is no guard of any other kind. On delete the code does three
things: it sets `organizationId` to `None` on every market of that organization, it `$pull`s the
org id from every user's `organizations` array, and it deletes the organization document. Nothing
is refused, nothing is confirmed beyond the modal, and nothing is recoverable.

**Why that is worth thinking about, not just patching:**

- **It manufactures a state the product refuses to create.** `POST /markets` rejects a payload with
  no `organizationId` - a market with no organization cannot be *made*, yet this makes them by the
  handful. Every reader downstream was written against an invariant that this one call breaks.
- **It silently changes who can see a market.** `get_markets_for_user` reaches a market two ways:
  an explicit role on the market, or membership of its organization. An orphaned market keeps its
  explicit-role holders and loses everyone who reached it through the org - no notice, no trace.
- **A live market can be orphaned.** Nothing checks the phase. An organization holding a published
  market with a public check-in URL and vendors assigned for market day deletes exactly as easily
  as an empty one.
- **There is no undo and no record.** `placement_history` covers placements only; an organization's
  deletion is not written anywhere.

**The two questions, which are genuinely separate:**

1. **What should a market do when its organization goes away?** Candidates: refuse the delete while
   markets exist; require the markets be moved to another organization first; cascade-delete them;
   keep orphaning but make an orphaned market a first-class state the whole product understands
   (who owns it, who sees it, whether it can still be edited, how it gets re-homed).
2. **What conditions should permit deleting an organization at all?** Owner-only is the current
   answer and it is about *authority*, not *safety*. Should it also depend on state - no markets,
   no non-draft markets, no other members, an explicit typed confirmation, a grace period?

**Note:** the answer to 2 may make 1 mostly moot (if deletion is refused while any market exists,
orphaning never happens), which is why they should be decided together rather than in sequence.

### Q3 - What should the create-new-market modal be?

**Screen:** the create-new-market overlay. `front-end/src/views/NewMarketOverlay.vue`.

**The ask:** rethink the modal's aesthetics and organization entirely, so it is clearer and more
attractive. Not a patch to what is there.

**What it is today:** a title (`Create new market`), an organization select, a market name field, a
Cancel / Create pair of buttons, an error line, and a close button. Two inputs and two buttons, in
one undifferentiated column.

**Why a redesign rather than a fix:** this is the first thing an organizer does in the product, and
it is currently the least considered screen in it - finding 03 is a field with `all: unset` and no

also sets the expectation for every other dialog: `ManageOrgOverlay`, `ManageMarketOverlay`,
`PlacementDialog` and the market-archive confirmation are all the same shape, so whatever is
decided here is effectively the product's dialog idiom.

**Questions the redesign has to answer:**

- **What does creating a market actually need?** Only a name and an organization are required
  today. Should the dialog ask for more (dates, intake mode) so the market starts further along, or
  deliberately stay minimal and hand off to the market workspace?
- **What does it look like when the organizer has exactly one organization?** The select is then a
  control with no choice in it. And when they have none - `new-market-org.spec.ts` already pins a
  zero-org fallback, which the redesign must keep working.
- **Where do errors go?** `.error-message` is currently absolutely positioned at `top: 35px;
  left: 50%`, which is a value chosen against a layout, not a rule.
- **Is a modal even right for this?** A full screen would have room to say what a market is and
  what happens next. A modal cannot.

**Related:** finding 03 is inside this dialog. If Q3 is taken up, 03 is answered by the redesign;
if Q3 sits, 03 should be fixed on its own because a field with no border is a defect either way.

**Precedent to follow:** `docs/design-system.md` is the single statement of what this product looks
like, and `front-end/src/assets/primitives.css` owns the controls. A redesign that invents new
values rather than composing those is the thing `E16` exists to stop.

### Q4 - Should the draft state be a guided, step-by-step workflow?

**Screen:** the market workspace, Market Setup tab, while the market is in `draft`.
`front-end/src/views/MarketSetupView.vue`.

**The ask:** the draft state presents too much at once, and the plan cards are too small for the
information and controls they have to hold. Turn the draft state into a step-by-step guided
workflow that lives *inside* the draft state - not a replacement for the workspace, and not a new
phase.

**What it is today:** all six plan cards are on screen simultaneously, laid out in three rows
(`--single`, `--triple`, `--asymmetric`), with the Application Form on a separate tab. Every
decision an organizer makes about a market is visible at once, in cards sized by whatever fits.

**The cards being too small is documented in the source, not just felt.** The comment above
`.plan-row--triple` records the previous round of this: equal thirds gave Section Setup - which
needs 654px for four columns plus a delete control - the same 460px as Location Setup, which needs
278px, and the consequence was the Tier select rendering 65px wide so every tier read `Pr...`,
`St...`, `Co...` on the field that sets a vendor's price. The current `0.78fr / 0.69fr / 1.53fr`
split is a negotiated truce, not a solution. A step-by-step workflow would give each card the whole
width, which dissolves the constraint rather than rebalancing it.

**Questions this has to answer:**

- **What are the steps, and in what order?** **Q5 answers this** - resolve it first. In short: the
  plan (minus Assignment Priority and Assignment Options) comes before the application form, which
  is built from it; and the two assignment cards move out of setup entirely, into a new stage
  between Applications and Assignment Results.
- **Where does the Application Form fit?** It is a tab today, but `FormHasFieldsGuard` blocks
  `draft -> applications_open` without it, so it is part of getting a market ready and arguably part
  of the same walk.
- **Is it linear or resumable?** An organizer will leave a draft and come back. The workflow must
  be re-enterable at any step, and must not force a walk-through to change one value later.
- **What does it become once the market leaves draft?** The plan stays editable in later phases.
  Does the workspace revert to the all-cards view then, or does the guided form persist? Two
  different layouts for the same data is a maintenance cost that needs to be chosen deliberately.
- **How does it relate to the phase rail?** The rail already communicates progress *between*
  phases. A second progress indicator *within* draft risks reading as a competing lifecycle - the
  two have to be visually distinguishable.

**Related:** Q1 (the Market Dates control) is one of these steps and should be decided inside this
question rather than beside it. Findings 04, 06 and 07 are all fixes to the current all-cards
layout; they are worth doing regardless, because the plan cards survive in some form either way.

**Precedent:** the floorplan wizard (`front-end/src/components/floorplan/`) is already a multi-step
guided workflow in this codebase. Whatever is decided here should either reuse its shape or
deliberately depart from it - two unrelated wizard idioms in one product is the `E16` mistake in a
new place.

### Q5 - What is the real order of stages in getting a market ready?

**Screen:** the market workspace as a whole - the tab bar, the phase rail and the plan cards
together.

**The correction this records:** the workspace presents the four tabs as peers and the six plan
cards as one simultaneous block, but the work has a genuine order, and two of the plan cards do not
belong where they sit.

**The order, as stated:**

1. **Market setup** - dates, tiers, locations, sections, **and table types**. **Minus Assignment
   Priority and Assignment Options**, which are not part of setting up a market.
2. **Application form** - built *from* the values decided in step 1.
3. **Finalize** both, which is what opens applications.
4. **Applications** - receive and review them.
5. **Assignment** - a stage that does not exist today, sitting between Applications and Assignment
   Results. This is where **Assignment Priority** and **Assignment Options** live.
6. **Assignment results.**

**The back end already agrees with steps 1 and 2 and says so.** `back-end/essential_fields.py`
opens with: *"The offering is never an independent list: it is the market plan itself. Dates come
from `SetupObject.market_dates`, sections from `SetupObject.sections`, tiers from
`SetupObject.tiers`, and table types from the market's floorplan."* The form's essential questions
cannot offer anything until the plan has been decided, so a workspace that shows the two tabs as
equal choices is showing a dependency as a preference. The tab order (Application Form first, then
Market Setup) actively inverts it.

**Table type belongs in step 1, and today it is nowhere.** Table type is the fourth essential
question (`essential_table_type_ranking`), and the form builder needs its options the same way it
needs dates, sections and tiers. But `essential_fields.py:124-129` records that table type is a
property of an individual TABLE, not of a section, so only a floorplan can describe it - and the
floorplan GUI is cut from MVP. The stand-in is `STUB_TABLE_TYPE = "Standard"`: every market offers
exactly one type, the fewer-than-two rule suppresses the ranking, and the question is never asked
of anybody.

So there is no place in the product where an organizer decides their table types, and the form
builder is asking a question whose options nobody authored. **Deciding table types belongs in the
market setup stage, before the form builder** - alongside dates, tiers, locations and sections -
which also removes the form's dependency on a floorplan that MVP does not ship. That means either a
Table Type plan card beside the others, or an explicit decision that the stub stands for MVP and
the question is deferred. What must not persist is the current middle ground, where the dependency
exists, the question exists, and the input does not.

**And steps 5 and 6 have the matching dependency in the other direction.** A priority rule names a
form field key or an `application.` attribute, so Assignment Priority cannot be filled in before
the form exists - and it is most meaningful once applications are in hand. Today it sits in Market
Setup, which is the earliest stage, i.e. the furthest possible point from where it belongs.

**Questions this has to answer:**

- **Is "Assignment" a new phase, a new tab, or a section of the existing Assignment Results tab?**
  Adding a phase means `VALID_TRANSITIONS`, `guards.py` and `_validate_registry()`, and the phase
  rail derives its spine from that table - so a new phase appears on the rail automatically, which
  may be exactly right or may be too heavy. A new tab is much cheaper and says less.
- **What exactly does "finalized" mean for step 3, and what writes it?** See finding 11:
  `published_at` exists, is read in two places, and is never assigned. Is finalizing one act over
  both the plan and the form, or two?
- **Can an organizer go back?** Step 1 feeding step 2 means editing the plan after the form is
  built changes what the form offers - which `essential_fields.py` already handles by freezing the
  offering at the first applicant answer. The workflow has to make that freeze visible rather than
  letting an organizer discover it.
- **What happens to the tab bar?** If the stages are ordered, four peer tabs is the wrong control
  for them. This may collapse into Q4's guided workflow rather than remaining a tab bar at all.

**Related:** Q4 (a guided step-by-step draft workflow) is the same question seen from the layout
side - Q5 says what the steps *are*, Q4 says how they are presented. They should be resolved
together, and Q5 first. Finding 11 (the landing tab) is downstream of both.

### Q6 - Which fields matter for review, who says so, and how does the review UI use that?

**Screens:** the form builder (`front-end/src/components/application/FormFieldEditor.vue`) and the
review queue (`front-end/src/components/application/ApplicationMonitor.vue`).

**The ask:** work out the relationship between an application's fields and what a reviewer actually
needs, give the form builder a way to mark the review-relevant ones, and have the reviewing UI pull
those out and present them per application.

**What exists today, and it is a heuristic standing in for exactly this.** The review card renders
`reviewAnswers()` (`front-end/src/utils/reviewQueue.ts:17`), which is every answer the application
carries, in one flat `<dl>` - **custom fields first, then essential answers**. The comment above it
states the reasoning: the organizer's own questions "are what distinguish applicants from each
other - what the vendor sells, their portfolio - while the essential answers mostly repeat, because
they are drawn from the same small offering."

So the product already believes some fields matter more for review than others. It just infers it
from *who authored the field* rather than being told. That inference is wrong in both directions: a
custom "How did you hear about us?" is noise on a triage card, and an essential availability answer
can be the whole decision.

**`FormField` has no field for this.** `back-end/datatypes.py:527` is `key`, `label`, `type`,
`required`, `options`, `help_text`, `order`. Adding one is a contract change - regenerate
`docs/schema.d.ts` with `python generate_market_schema.py --output ../docs/schema.d.ts`, pinned by
`tests/test_generate_market_schema.py`.

**Questions to settle:**

- **Is "review-relevant" a boolean, or an ordering?** A flag says which fields lead the card. A
  rank says what a reviewer reads first, second, third - which may matter more, since the complaint
  behind this is a flat dump.
- **Can essential answers be marked too, or only custom fields?** They are not `FormField`s - they
  are derived from the plan by `essential_fields.py` - so marking them needs a different mechanism
  than a property on a field. If only custom fields can be marked, the heuristic survives for half
  the card.
- **What happens to the unmarked fields?** Hidden, collapsed behind a disclosure, or still listed
  below the marked ones. A reviewer sometimes needs the rest; losing access to them outright is
  worse than the current dump.
- **The freeze is the sharp edge.** The application form locks once the first applicant submits
  (`application_form_lock_reason()`, D9) - and *when you learn which fields you actually needed for
  review is while reviewing*, which is after the lock. If review-relevance is stored on
  `applicationForm`, it becomes unchangeable at the exact moment it becomes knowable. That argues
  for holding it somewhere the lock does not reach, or for carving an explicit exception. Decide
  this before designing the UI, because it decides where the data lives.
- **Does `required` already mean some of this?** A required field is one the organizer insisted on.
  That is not the same as review-relevant, but the overlap needs stating so the builder does not
  end up with two checkboxes an organizer cannot tell apart.

**Related:** `SOLVER_RELEVANT_KEYS` (`back-end/essential_fields.py`) is a third, existing
classification of the same fields - "the answers a change to which invalidates a review". Three
overlapping notions of which answers matter (required, solver-relevant, review-relevant) is a
vocabulary problem before it is a UI problem; name them apart or unify them deliberately.

**Depends on:** Q5. Reviewing is step 4 in that order, and this question is about what step 4 shows.

### Q7 - What shape should the CSV import flow be?

**Screen:** Import applications from CSV. `front-end/src/views/CsvImportView.vue`.

**The ask:** the page carries a lot of white space. Decide what to do about it - a modal, centring,
or something else. Recorded as a future decision, not a fix.

**Read the file's own header before deciding.** `CsvImportView.vue` opens with: *"A full-width flow
rather than a dialog: mapping a dozen columns against a target list is too dense for one, and
Market Setup already carries dates, sections, tiers, priorities and the form builder."* It goes on
to record that the column-ledger shape *"won a three-variant prototype"*. So "make it a modal" is
not a fresh idea - it is a reversal of a decision that was prototyped and written down. That does
not make it wrong, but it has to be argued against what that prototype found, not around it.

**The white space has a concrete cause, and it is not the same on every step.**

- `.import-view` declares `padding` and no `max-width` **at all**. Every other screen in the product
  picks one of the two widths CLAUDE.md names - `--workspace-max` (1440) or `--list-max` (1100) -
  and `MarketsView`, `OrganizationsView`, `TablesView`, `VendorsView` and `AttendanceStatusView` all
  do. This view opted out of the sizing model rather than choosing within it.
- **Three of the four steps are narrow.** Upload, Preview and Confirm render `.import-panel`, which
  is `max-width: 720px` - a 720px panel stranded on an uncapped page is exactly the white space
  reported.
- **One step is wide.** Map columns renders `.import-map`, a grid of `minmax(0, 1fr)` plus a 280px
  rail, which genuinely wants the room.

So a single width for the whole wizard cannot be right: the step that needs width and the steps
that do not are in the same shell. That is the real question, and it is why this is a decision
rather than a `max-width` line.

**Options, none of them free:**

1. **Cap the view at `--workspace-max` and centre it.** Smallest change, keeps the recorded
   full-width decision, and leaves the three narrow steps still adrift inside 1440.
2. **Let each step choose its own width**, centred - narrow for upload/preview/confirm, wide for
   mapping. Honest to the content; means the shell resizes between steps, which can read as jumpy.
3. **Modal for the narrow steps, full page for mapping.** Reverses the header comment for three
   steps out of four and splits one flow across two presentations.
4. **Modal throughout**, as suggested. Directly contradicts the prototype finding; would need the
   ledger to work in a dialog, which is what that prototype says it does not.

**Related:** `.import-panel h2` and `.import-ledger h2` both use `--text-lg`, which the scale
reserves for "section headings, card titles" - that part is correct here. And CLAUDE.md's rule
that a screen "is one of two widths and never caps its own height" is the standard this view has to
be measured against; whatever is decided should put it back inside that model rather than beside it.

### Q8 - The form asks dates and tiers one way; real CSVs ask them several ways

**Contract:** `back-end/essential_fields.py` (the applicant validator) against
`back-end/csv_import.py` (the import boundary). Front-end mirrors:
`front-end/src/utils/essentialFields.ts`, the form builder, the applicant form.

**The ask:** the form should be flexible about how availability and tier preference are asked, and
that flexibility must stay backwards compatible with the CSV upload path.

**The asymmetry, stated precisely - and it runs the opposite way to what you would guess.**

**CSV import is already flexible.** `_assembled_rows` (`csv_import.py:780-800`) accepts *two*
shapes and normalises both at the boundary, with the reasoning in place:

- **A per-date grid** - one column per market date, tiers in the cell. That single grid answers
  *both* questions, so availability is read from it ("the dates you named tiers for are the dates
  you are available") rather than demanding a second column the form never had. `unserved_required`
  knows this too, and the comment records that it used to be decided in two places where only one
  of them knew.
- **A flat list** - a form that asked once ("which tiers will you accept?") gives one list, widened
  to those tiers on every date the vendor is available.

**The applicant form is not flexible.** `essential_fields.py`'s tier validator accepts a `dict`
only, and requires a non-empty entry for **every** available date - *"'Tier preference' is missing
for {date}. Choose at least one tier for every date you are available, or remove that date."* There
is one input method, and a CSV whose real shape differs has nowhere to land on the form side.

**So the thing to preserve is already proven: storage stays canonical, input methods vary.** Both
CSV shapes converge on one stored answer - `{date: [tier, ...]}`, tiers in the market plan's order -
before anything downstream reads them. Whatever input methods the form gains should normalise at
*its* boundary the same way, so the solver, the review card, the applicant dashboard and the
importer keep reading exactly one shape. Backwards compatibility means the storage contract does
not move; it does not mean the form keeps its one control.

**Questions to settle:**

- **Which input methods does the form offer, and who chooses?** Per-date tier grid, one flat tier
  question plus a separate dates question, or both. Is that an organizer choice in the form
  builder, or does the shape follow the plan (one date, one tier -> do not ask a grid)?
- **What does the form builder show for this?** Today the essential questions panel has one toggle,
  "Ask this", on section preference only. An input-method choice is a different kind of control and
  needs its own treatment - see finding 12 and Q6, which both touch that panel.
- **Where does normalisation live so it is not written twice?** `csv_import.py` has its widening
  logic inline. If the form gains the same shapes, that logic wants to be one function both call.
  CLAUDE.md's rule for this contract is that `essential_fields.py` is the single owner and
  `essentialFields.ts` its mirror; a third copy in the import path is the drift to avoid.
- **What is actually incompatible about the CSV in hand?** This note records the structural gap.
  The specific failure should be reproduced against the real file before designing, per the
  reproduce-first rule - the importer handles more shapes than it looks like, so the failing case
  may be narrower or wider than "dates and tiers".

**Related:** Q5 puts market setup before the form, which is what makes the offering (dates, tiers)
known when the form is built. Q6 and finding 12 are the other two open items on the same panel.
