# Every approved vendor gets a table by the market's rules

The assignment seats the market's approved vendors, read from their applications, by the organizer's rules and the vendors' own answers.
Nobody is placed at a tier they refused, on a day they cannot come, or past their limit; two who asked to share a table sit together; and the same market assigns the same way every time.
A vendor left without a table on a date is told why, in words the organizer can read.
Before the run an organizer can give a vendor a seat that the run keeps, and after it can move or free anyone by hand, with a record of who changed what.
The result says when the rules, the plan or the approved applications have changed since it ran, and the rules close once the market can no longer run it again.

## Why

A table is what a vendor applied for, and the organizer must be able to stand behind every seat: to the vendor in it, and to the vendor without one.
An assignment that leaves tables free and vendors unplaced with no reason on screen cannot be defended, and an organizer cannot tell a fault from a full room.
Real markets change on the morning itself, when vendors drop out or arrive unannounced, so a seat must be changeable by hand without running everything again.
And the assignment reads the applications themselves, so whichever way vendors came in, nothing has to be fabricated to reach it.
