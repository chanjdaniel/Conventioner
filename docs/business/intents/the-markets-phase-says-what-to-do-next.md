# The market's phase says what to do next

A market is always in exactly one phase, and the phase is the one model of where it stands and what may happen to it.
At every phase the organizer sees what to do now and what comes next without knowing the product: the phase rail names the one next step, says before the click why it cannot be taken yet, offers the fix as its main button, and once the step is taken lands them on the page where the next work happens.
Each intake path reads as its own sequence, in its own words, over the same phases.
The setup is settled before vendors arrive, so nothing they answered can drift under them.

## Why

When the setup screens kept a second model of where a market stood, the two disagreed and broke the plainest path through the product.
The work of running a market has a real order, and a market that shows every screen at once, offering what it cannot do yet and refusing only after the click, leaves the organizer to discover that order by failing.
An organizer who already holds their responses should not be asked to open and close applications they never took.
And vendors answer against the market's dates, tiers and sections, so a plan that changed after they answered would make their answers wrong without anyone touching them.
